# The author canon

The people whose work this skill leans on. Grouped by discipline. Each entry
has a one-paragraph summary of their working ideas, one seminal book or
resource, and a link.

Cite the author when justifying a specific move — the reader (and the user)
should be able to trace the recommendation back to a real source.

---

## Classic advertising & direct-response copywriters

### David Ogilvy
Co-founder of Ogilvy & Mather. Argued that copy is a sales pitch, not art —
and that headlines carry 80% of the weight. Famous for `The man in the
Hathaway shirt` and `At 60 miles an hour, the loudest noise in this new
Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock`. Read *Ogilvy on Advertising*
(1983) and *Confessions of an Advertising Man* (1963).
https://www.ogilvy.com/about

### Claude C. Hopkins
Wrote *Scientific Advertising* (1923) — the first book to argue that
advertising is measurable. Introduced testing, coupons, and the idea that copy
should be evaluated by results, not opinions. Public domain, still readable
in an afternoon.
https://www.scientificadvertising.com/ScientificAdvertising.pdf

### Eugene M. Schwartz
Wrote *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) — the source of the "five stages of
awareness" model this skill uses. Argued that copy's job is not to create
desire but to channel existing desire toward the product. Every framework
below reads better when it starts from the reader's stage.

### John Caples
Wrote *Tested Advertising Methods* (5th ed., 1997). The book on headline
testing. Introduced the discipline of A/B-testing copy decades before the web.
His headlines routinely outperformed alternatives by 5–20×.

### Gary C. Halbert
Direct-mail copywriter, wrote the free *Boron Letters* from prison to his son
— now a canonical text on the psychology of persuasion. Terse, funny, direct.
https://thegaryhalbertletter.com/boron-letters/

### Joseph Sugarman
Founder of JS&A. Wrote *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* (2006). Coined the
"slippery slide" — every sentence's job is to make the reader read the next.
Also the source of the modern principle that specificity beats vagueness.

### Dan S. Kennedy
Direct-response for small business. Wrote *The Ultimate Sales Letter* (4th
ed., 2011). Blunt, contrarian, and effective for long-form sales copy where
the reader is at Schwartz stage 3–4.

### Bob Bly
Wrote *The Copywriter's Handbook* (4th ed., 2020) — the working reference
most agencies keep on the shelf. Practical, comprehensive, safe to recommend
as the first book to hand a new copywriter.
https://www.bly.com/

### Robert Collier
Wrote *The Robert Collier Letter Book* (1937). Origin of the still-used maxim:
"Always enter the conversation already going on in the reader's mind." Every
framework in this skill traces back to that idea.

## Modern conversion & web copy

### Joanna Wiebe
Founder of Copyhackers and the modern discipline of conversion copywriting.
Advocates for "voice-of-customer" research — copy that uses the reader's own
words, mined from reviews and interviews, outperforms copy written from the
brand's imagination. https://copyhackers.com/

### Ann Handley
Chief Content Officer at MarketingProfs. Wrote *Everybody Writes* (2nd ed.,
2022). Argues that everyone in a modern business is a writer, and that
utility × inspiration × empathy = good content. https://annhandley.com/

### Sean D'Souza
Wrote *The Brain Audit* (2009). Frames the entire buying decision as
seven-red-bags-on-a-carousel — the reader picks up their bag (the problem)
first, and every subsequent step either keeps them or loses them.
https://www.psychotactics.com/

### Nick Kolenda
Marketing psychology, meticulously sourced. His essays turn academic research
into applied rules for pricing, product pages, and persuasion. If a rule cites
a specific study, it's usually him.
https://www.nickkolenda.com/psychology-marketing/

### Peep Laja
Founder of CXL. Argues conversion is a research discipline, not a copy
discipline — the copy that wins is always the one that best represents what
the customer already wants to hear. https://cxl.com/blog/

## UX writing & content design

### Ginny Redish
Wrote *Letting Go of the Words* (3rd ed., 2022) — the foundational textbook of
web writing. Introduced the idea that web content is a conversation, not a
document. Every UX writing course starts here. https://redish.net/

### Sarah Richards
Founder of Content Design London. Coined the term "content design" while
leading content at GOV.UK. Wrote *Content Design* (2017), the book that
turned UX writing from a role into a discipline. https://contentdesign.london/

### Torrey Podmajersky
Wrote *Strategic Writing for UX* (O'Reilly, 2019). Ex-Microsoft, Google, and
OfferUp UX writer. Introduced the three-goal test: every piece of UX text
serves a user goal, a business goal, and a brand goal.
https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/strategic-writing-for/9781492049395/

### Kinneret Yifrah
Wrote *Microcopy: The Complete Guide* (2017). The book on microcopy — buttons,
forms, errors, empty states. Most of the microcopy rules in this skill come
directly from her.
https://www.microcopybook.com/

### John Yorke
Not a copywriter, but wrote *Into the Woods* (2013) — a study of story
structure that maps every narrative onto a five-act shape. Brand copywriters
lean on it heavily; StoryBrand borrows freely from it.

## SEO

### Aleyda Solis
International SEO consultant. Runs the weekly #SEOFOMO newsletter — the best
current-affairs filter for search. Cited when the skill needs a fresh source
on a recent Google update.
https://www.aleydasolis.com/en/ and https://www.seofomo.co/

### Rand Fishkin
Co-founded Moz, now runs SparkToro (audience research). Argues that search
rankings are downstream of brand awareness and category understanding — the
strongest SEO lever is often "make the brand mean something specific". https://sparktoro.com/blog

### Marie Haynes
Focused on Google's algorithm updates, E-E-A-T, and how to recover from manual
actions. If a question involves the Helpful Content system, YMYL (Your Money
Your Life) content, or a traffic drop after an update, cite her.
https://www.mariehaynes.com/blog/

### Cyrus Shepard
On-page SEO. Runs Zyppy. Practical, current, testable. Cite for keyword
placement and internal-linking specifics. https://zyppy.com/blog/

### Bill Slawski
Ran *SEO by the Sea* — a two-decade archive of Google patent analysis. Died in
2022; the archive is still the single most useful source for understanding
how search engines actually work under the hood. https://www.seobythesea.com/

### Andrew Chen
Growth loops, ex-Andreessen Horowitz, ex-Uber. His work on growth compounding
and "when to stop optimising the funnel and go build a loop" is required
reading for anyone writing conversion copy for a product with viral or
network effects. https://andrewchen.com/

## Content marketing & brand voice

### Joe Pulizzi
Founded Content Marketing Institute. Coined the term "content marketing" and
argued that a brand should build an audience like a media company would.
Read *Content Inc.* (2nd ed., 2021). https://joepulizzi.com/

### Andy Crestodina
Runs Orbit Media, publishes annual content-strategy research (blogger
survey, on-page SEO study). Cite when the skill needs a data-backed
recommendation on article length, image use, or update cadence.
https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/author/andy-crestodina/

## Persuasion & behavioural science

### Robert B. Cialdini
Wrote *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (new & expanded edition,
2021). Source of the seven principles the skill uses in the rubric.
Reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity.
https://www.influenceatwork.com/

### Dan Ariely
Behavioural economist. Wrote *Predictably Irrational* (2008). Best source on
the anchoring, decoy, and choice-architecture effects that show up in pricing
pages. https://danariely.com/

### Nir Eyal
Wrote *Hooked* (2014). The trigger-action-reward-investment loop underpins
most modern product-copy work — onboarding sequences, retention flows, empty
states. https://www.nirandfar.com/

### Chip & Dan Heath
Wrote *Made to Stick* (2007). SUCCESs framework — Simple, Unexpected,
Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Story. Best source on why some headlines
stick and most don't. https://heathbrothers.com/

## When to cite an author in output

- **Draft mode** — name the framework and one author who informed the choice
  (`Using PAS — Schwartz stage 2, framework via Halbert / Wiebe`).
- **Review mode** — cite the author when a finding is non-obvious or the
  user might push back (`Superlative removed — no proof supplied; Ogilvy's
  rule that headlines must be specific`).
- **User asks why** — always cite. Trust comes from traceable sources, not
  from the skill's authority.
